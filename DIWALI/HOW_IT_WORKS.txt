HOW IT WORKS - Diwali
rajeet-04

The whole artwork is one Python file. It works in six steps.

1. Where you stand
   The camera sits low by the water and looks up into a black sky.
   It drifts a little, like someone watching, and lifts slightly when
   the greeting appears. Everything is projected in true perspective,
   so near shells look bigger and far ones smaller.

2. The sky shots
   Every shell is planned once: when it launches, where, how high it
   bursts, its colours and its kind. A rising shell follows a ballistic
   arc (it slows under gravity and stops exactly at its burst height),
   with a hot white head, a gold tail and sparks shed behind it.

3. The bursts
   Each burst throws stars outward. Their flight is solved exactly for
   air drag and gravity, so stars race out, slow down and droop.
   - peony: stars spread evenly over a sphere (a golden-angle spiral)
   - chrysanthemum: the same sphere with long glowing streaks
   - ring: a tilted circle with a small core of another colour
   - willow: heavy drag, long gold trails that hang and crackle
   - palm: a few thick arms reaching up and bending over
   Stars change colour late in their life and strobe as they burn out.
   The show builds up, ends in a fan of shells, then quiets.

4. The greeting
   "Happy" in italics over spaced capitals "DIWALI", with a small
   flourish, is turned into thousands of points in the sky. One large
   shell bursts, and its stars fly along curved paths that overshoot and
   settle onto the letters, writing them from left to right. The letters
   shimmer, glints run over them, and then they break apart into
   falling embers.

5. The city and the water
   A city skyline with two temple domes sits across the water. Windows
   glow, strings of little lamps run along the rooftops with a light
   chasing through them, and every burst briefly lights the buildings.
   Every spark is mirrored in the water with a gentle ripple.

6. From points to letters
   The screen is a grid of letter cells. Solid things (the city and the
   greeting) are drawn nearest-first, with outlines  | / \ - _  where
   they meet the sky. A spark is drawn by how bright it is ( . : + * );
   a moving spark becomes a short stroke along its direction ( - / | \ );
   reflections become  ~ . Each burst opens with a white star and rays.
   Each cell is drawn as a small glyph in its colour, the previous
   frame fades behind it for trails, a soft glow is added, and the
   picture updates up to 30 times a second.
